## Design Specificity Verdict

**Mixed — copy carries specificity, the visual system doesn't, and the two disagree with each other on audience.**

LLM assessment: Positive signals: "dojang" terminology, instructor rank field, Korean-flag palette tied to Hapkido's origin, timetable variants literally named kids/youth/advanced/adults, zero tournament/competition language anywhere in hardcoded copy. Negative signals: `CardGridPage.tsx` is reused verbatim for About/Classes/Locations/Instructors — strip the color tokens and it's a generic template. `globals.css` loads only system Arial/Helvetica, no custom typeface. Most importantly, the site's own metadata contradicts its own hero: `layout.tsx` promises "children, youth, and adults" and a "supportive family environment"; `HeroSection.tsx` delivers only "Helping Children Build Confidence & Discipline Through Hapkido."

Deterministic scan: `detect.mjs --json app components` returned 1 finding: `side-tab` rule on `components/content/markdownComponents.tsx:36` (`border-l-4` on a `<blockquote>`). Verified against source — **likely false positive**: the rule targets thick colored borders on dashboard-style *cards* mimicking AI-slop UI; this is a conventional typographic blockquote treatment for CMS-rendered markdown, not a card.

Visual overlays: not available this session (see below).

## Overall Impression

The traditional-values, family-focused DNA is genuinely present in the codebase (age-tiered contact form, footer tagline, instructor copy, FAQ safety category) — it just isn't surfaced where it counts. The single biggest opportunity: promote what already exists in the footer/form/instructor copy up into the hero and above-the-fold content, rather than inventing new messaging.

## What's Working

1. Consistent, restrained visual system — never drifts into aggressive gym-bro tone, zero competitive/tournament language in hardcoded copy.
2. All-ages/traditional-values structural bones already exist: contact form's 5-tier age ladder, timetable's age/level color variants, FAQ's 5-category taxonomy including "Safety."
3. Above-average operational UX care: contact form's inline validation + success state, and a genuinely helpful 404 page with three contextual recovery links.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Hero and WhyChoose copy erase two of three named audiences**
Why it matters: `HeroSection.tsx` and `WhyChooseSection.tsx` speak only to parents of young children, directly contradicting `layout.tsx`'s own "children, youth, and adults" promise — signals "not for you" to teen-parents and adults in the first 3 seconds.
Fix: Rewrite H1 and WhyChoose copy to name/imply all three age segments.
Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P1] No explicit "traditional, not sport" statement — and Contact page's schema.org type says the opposite**
Why it matters: `app/contact/page.tsx` sets `"@type": "SportsActivityLocation"` in JSON-LD; no page states the personal-development/non-competitive differentiator directly. This directly cuts against the client's explicit instruction.
Fix: Add one explicit sentence naming the non-sport, personal-development positioning; correct the JSON-LD type.
Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P1] Testimonials funnel visitors off-site at peak intent**
Why it matters: "View More Reviews" links to an external Google search results page — sends convinced visitors away from the funnel into a surface where competitor ads can appear.
Fix: Surface more testimonials in-page or repoint the CTA to reinforce on-site conversion.
Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Member training calendar interrupts the homepage's persuasive sequence**
Why it matters: Internal jargon ("Weekly Themes," "gradings") with zero conversion value, sitting between Programs and Locations, breaks single-focus flow.
Fix: Move below the fold or off the homepage entirely.
Suggested command: `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] The site's best all-ages asset — the age ladder — is buried in a form dropdown**
Why it matters: The precise Little Tigers/Children/Youth/Adults tiers only appear after a visitor commits to filling out the contact form — too late to help someone still deciding.
Fix: Surface the same age bands near the Hero or as the Programs section's organizing structure.
Suggested command: `/impeccable layout`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (confused first-timer researching for a teen/adult):** Meets a child-only Hero and WhyChoose (two full sections of doubt) before seeing age-labeled classes in the Programs carousel; risks being pulled off-site by "View More Reviews" before reaching the FAQ, which isn't in primary nav.

**Casey (distracted mobile user):** Gets a sticky mobile CTA (good), but the entire trust-signal visual block ("Safe & Structured," "Traditional Martial Arts" floating cards) is wrapped in `hidden lg:block` — Casey never sees either reassurance micro-card that desktop users get.

**Priya (parent of a 7-year-old comparing 3 local schools — project-specific persona):** The current Hero is actually built for her and mostly works — H1 matches her need, stats read credible, WhyChoose maps to her screening criteria. Her friction points: the irrelevant calendar (scroll fatigue) and the Google-reviews detour at her moment of highest intent.

## Minor Observations

- No custom typeface loaded (`globals.css` — system Arial/Helvetica only) despite `next/font` making this trivial; undercuts brand distinctiveness.
- FAQ and Contact both absent from `config/navigation.ts`'s primary array (Contact has a CTA-button substitute; FAQ has none).
- Kids=blue/Youth=red timetable coding has no visitor-inferable rationale; a belt-color-inspired scheme would speak more directly to martial-arts convention.
- `not-found.tsx` is unusually well-built (three contextual recovery cards, dual CTAs) — preserve as-is.

## Questions to Consider

- Does the internal training calendar need to sit in the first-time visitor's primary scroll path, or was it placed there for CMS/staff convenience?
